[chordotomy](/projects/chordotomy/) listens to a recording and writes out its chords, beat by beat, with Roman numerals. When I first measured it on September 30, it named the right major or minor triad for barely half the time on a solo-guitar dataset. By October 2 that was 0.900. Each of the five steps in between paid for its points with something else.

## How it's measured

Two public datasets, scored with mir_eval. Tiny AAM is 20 mixed tracks, annotated in major and minor only. GuitarSet is 180 solo-guitar accompaniment takes, full of sevenths and jazz voicings.

The main number is majmin, the share of time where the analyzer names the right major or minor triad. Below, a "point" is 0.01 of a score.

| | Tiny AAM | GuitarSet |
|---|---|---|
| Start: DSP only | 0.738 | 0.519 |
| 1. Whitening | 0.788 | 0.619 |
| 2. A bigger vocabulary | 0.793 | 0.661 |
| 3. A pretrained model | 0.913 | 0.787 |
| 4. The bass | 0.934 | 0.872 |
| 5. The beats | **0.950** | **0.900** |

One footnote. After step 3, I found a bug in my own scoring: it stretched Tiny AAM's last chord over each file's silent tail. Fixing it lifted every Tiny AAM chord score by about a point, with no change to the analyzer. From step 4 on, Tiny AAM is scored the fixed way.

## Where it started: "no chord" won too often

The first version was textbook signal processing. Fold the spectrum into a 12-note chroma, compare it with a template for each chord, and add a flat template for "no chord".

On real mixes, "no chord" won far too often. It covered 12% of Tiny AAM, where the annotations have 1.5%, and 77% of one track. A full mix's chroma is never sparse enough for a triad template to beat a flat line.

## 1. Whitening: 0.519 → 0.619

The fix came from Mauch and Dixon's 2010 paper: whiten each bin of the spectrum against a running one-octave background, so the notes stand out from the mush around them. Their plugin is GPL, so I implemented it from the paper. "No chord" also stopped being a template. A chord now has to clear a bar, and beats far quieter than the loud ones go to "no chord". The bass register got its own chroma, which votes for the chords whose tones it holds.

This step also set a rule that held for every step after it: a suite of synthesized test clips has to pass, whatever the scores say. Two-beat chord changes and first-inversion chords must survive. Without that constraint, Tiny AAM could have reached about 0.823. With it, 0.788. I took the 0.788 and wrote the difference down in the docs.

## 2. A bigger vocabulary: 0.619 → 0.661

Up to here the analyzer knew three chord types: major, minor and dominant seventh. Anything else had to be named by the nearest of those, often the wrong one. I added major and minor sevenths, minor sixth, half-diminished seventh, diminished seventh and sus4. The templates also learned each note's overtones, its octave, twelfth and double octave, so a triad's own overtones stop looking like a seventh. The overtones alone were worth 2 to 3 points on GuitarSet. GuitarSet's majmin reached 0.661, and its sevenths score went from 0.492 to 0.531.

Tiny AAM paid for it. Annotated in major and minor only, it scores a sus4 as a wrong triad and a seventh as a wrong seventh chord, and it gave up 0.9 points of root accuracy and 1.2 of its sevenths score. From here on the two datasets had different jobs. GuitarSet's sevenths was the score to push, and Tiny AAM's floors capped how much of the new vocabulary the decoder could use.

## 3. A pretrained model: 0.661 → 0.787

Next I scored the DSP side by side with lv-chordia, a pretrained chord recognizer (Jiang, Chen, Li and Xia, ISMIR 2019). It scored above the DSP on every metric, on both datasets, and the biggest jump in this table is its.

Before it went in, license and size mattered more than the score. A model whose weights' terms I can't state doesn't go in, and lv-chordia's authors committed its weights under MIT to the same repository as the code. Size was torch's problem: torch alone is over 500 MB. So the model lives in an optional extra, and the default install and the DSP path never import torch. A plain `pip install chordotomy` runs the DSP without a model. The README also states what the model was trained on, so anyone who would rather not use it can leave it out.

## 4. The bass: 0.787 → 0.872

chordotomy also reads the bass under each chord, so slash chords like C/E come out as such. That bass came from a simple DSP rule, and in crowded passages it grabbed the wrong low note. A bass outside the chord is scored as an extra note in it, so false slash chords were quietly costing chord accuracy.

Two changes fixed it. A tied vote over a chord's beats now prefers a chord tone. And lv-chordia turned out to have a separate output just for the bass, which my code computed and never read. Now that output judges any bass outside the chord.

GuitarSet's majmin rose to 0.872 and its sevenths to 0.819, but not because the analyzer heard chords better. Both numbers match, to three decimals, what step 3 scored on the chord names alone, with the bass left out. The chord names didn't change; the false extra notes went away. The bass itself did get better: the share of beats with the right bass rose from 0.49 to 0.61. The cost was some real inversions. I let inversion recall fall by at most a tenth, and the bass output took it from 0.227 to 0.208.

How a single song turned up this bug, along with a 7sus4 bug the benchmarks couldn't see, is [its own post](/blog/the-model-already-knew/).

## 5. The beats: 0.872 → 0.900

lv-chordia labels short frames, and chordotomy snaps the labels to beats. That snap scored below the model's own frame output, by 1.3 points on Tiny AAM and 2.1 on GuitarSet. Was snapping the problem?

No. The same snap onto the datasets' annotated beats scored above the raw model. The loss was librosa's beat tracker, which got only 72 of GuitarSet's 180 takes right.

Beat This! (Foscarin, Schlüter and Widmer, ISMIR 2024) tracks beats much better, but the beats it picks itself aren't evenly spaced. Over chords struck once a bar it leaves gaps of up to 1.84 s. So I kept librosa's steady-tempo tracker and fed it Beat This!'s beat probability instead of librosa's onset strength:

```python
envelope = beats.activation(y)  # Beat This!'s beat probability per frame
tempo, frames = librosa.beat.beat_track(
    onset_envelope=envelope, sr=SR, hop_length=HOP, trim=False
)
```

GuitarSet's beat accuracy (beat F) went from 0.517 to 0.919. The snapped chords now beat the raw model on both datasets: 0.950 against 0.947 on Tiny AAM, and 0.900 against 0.893 on GuitarSet.

It wasn't free either. 21 of the 180 takes lost majmin, the worst of them fast jazz takes that Beat This! tracks at half tempo, and inversion recall slipped to 0.191, under the floor from step 4. I traced that to the beats moving about 30 ms closer to the annotated ones, which changed which bass note holds for two beats. majmin_inv, which scores the bass too, rose from 0.592 to 0.611, so I accepted it.

One honesty check. Beat This! was trained on GuitarSet, so GuitarSet's numbers here use checkpoints that never trained on the take being scored, though they had heard other takes of the same tune. The model that ships trained on all 180 takes and scores 0.902.

The DSP engine from the starting point is still there. It picked up the bass step's fixes too and now scores 0.803 on Tiny AAM and 0.696 on GuitarSet; `--engine dsp` picks it for anyone who'd rather skip the model. Both engines ship in one package on PyPI (`uv tool install "chordotomy[model]"`), and the [viewer](https://zeikar.dev/chordotomy/) plays a recording with its chords.
